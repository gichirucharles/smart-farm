## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Cambridge IGCSE (International General Certificate of Secondary Education) is offered by premium international schools in Kenya, serving students in Years 10-11 (Grades 9-10 equivalent). Unlike the national CBC and KCSE systems, IGCSE provides globally recognized qualifications with rigorous assessment methods combining external examinations and internal coursework. This report details the complete IGCSE framework for implementation in ShuleVerse.

## SECTION 2: IGCSE GRADING SYSTEM

### 2.1 Grading Scale (A* to G)
| Grade | Descriptor | Performance Level | Approximate % |
|-------|-----------|------------------|---------------|
| A* | Outstanding | 90-100% | Exceptional |
| A | Excellent | 80-89% | Very Strong |
| B | Good | 70-79% | Strong |
| C | Satisfactory | 60-69% | Competent |
| D | Acceptable | 50-59% | Adequate |
| E | Minimal | 40-49% | Basic |
| F | Poor | 30-39% | Limited |
| G | Very Poor | 0-29% | Insufficient |
| U | Ungraded | Below 0% | Not achieved |

### 2.2 Alternative 9-1 Grading (UK Administrative Zone)
- Available in administrative zone 3 (England, UK region)
- **NOT used in Kenya** - Kenyan IGCSE schools use traditional A*-G
- For reference: 9=A*, 8=A, 7=B, 6=C, 5=D, 4=E, 3=F, 2=G, 1=U

### 2.3 Grade Thresholds
- **Dynamic threshold system:** Thresholds vary per examination session
- Published after each exam series by Cambridge International
- Based on candidature difficulty and global performance
- Minimum pass grade: **E** (some universities require C or above)
- Distinction grade: **A*** (top universities/competitive programs)

### 2.4 Important Features
- **No ranking system** - Students receive absolute grades, not percentile rankings
- **Subject-specific thresholds** - Different subjects have different mark requirements
- **Moderation system** - Coursework marked by schools, moderated by Cambridge
- **Grade boundaries published** - After each examination session publicly available

## SECTION 4: IGCSE ASSESSMENT METHODS

### 4.1 Assessment Components Breakdown

#### **Paper-Based Examinations (External - Cambridge Marked)**
- **Weightage:** 60-72.5% of total grade (varies by subject)
- **Duration:** Typically 1-3 hours per paper
- **Number of Papers:** 2-4 papers per subject depending on subject
- **Content:** Structured questions, essays, problem-solving

#### **Coursework/Practical Components (Internal - School Marked, Cambridge Moderated)**
- **Weightage:** 27.5-40% of total grade (varies by subject)
- **Types:**
  - Practical experiments (Sciences)
  - Portfolio projects (Arts)
  - Written coursework (Languages, Humanities)
  - Programming tasks (Computer Science)
- **Moderation:** Sample submitted to Cambridge for verification

#### **Continuous Assessment Elements**
- **In-class assessments** - Teacher-recorded (NOT formally moderated)
- **Mock examinations** - Simulate real papers, diagnostic tool
- **Formative feedback** - Ongoing teacher guidance

### 4.2 Subject-Specific Assessment Breakdown

| Subject | Component 1 | Component 2 | Component 3 | Weighting |
|---------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Mathematics (0580)** | Paper 1 Non-calc | Paper 2 Calc | - | 50% each |
| **English Language (0511)** | Reading & Writing | Listening & Speaking | - | 50% each |
| **Biology (0610)** | Exam (72.5%) | Practical (27.5%) | - | Multiple papers |
| **Physics (0625)** | Exam (72.5%) | Practical (27.5%) | - | Multiple papers |
| **Computer Science (0984)** | Theory (60%) | Programming task (40%) | - | Combined |
| **Art & Design (0400)** | Portfolio (60%) | Exam (40%) | - | Combined |

### 4.3 Examination Schedule

#### **Main Examination Session (June/July)**
- Typically held in May-June
- Results released in August
- Majority of candidates worldwide

#### **Supplementary Session (Nov/Dec)**
- Limited subject availability
- For resits or late entries
- Results in January

#### **Alternative Session (March/April)**
- Specific administrative zones only
- Not widely available in Kenya

### 4.4 Question Types
- **Multiple Choice (MCQ)** - Objective questions
- **Short Answer** - 1-3 line responses
- **Structured Questions** - Multi-part calculation/analysis
- **Essay Questions** - Extended written responses
- **Practical Investigations** - Real lab work or simulations
- **Case Studies** - Application of knowledge to scenarios

## SECTION 6: KEY DIFFERENCES: IGCSE vs CBC vs KCSE

### 6.1 Comparative Analysis

| Factor | IGCSE | CBC (Grades 1-9) | KCSE (Grades 10-12) |
|--------|-------|------------------|------------------|
| **Grading System** | A*-G (8 grades) | 1-7 (7 levels) | A-E (5 grades) + Points |
| **Assessment Type** | External exams + coursework | Continuous assessment | High-stakes final exam |
| **Coursework Weight** | 27.5-40% | 100% school-based | 0% (exam-only) |
| **Focus** | Subject mastery, critical thinking | Competencies, skills | Academic knowledge |
| **Recognition** | Global (universities worldwide) | National (Kenyan unis only) | National (Kenyan unis) |
| **Duration** | 2 years (Years 10-11) | 8 years (Grades 1-8) | 3 years (Forms 1-4) |
| **Marks Conversion** | Set by Cambridge each session | Static levels (1-7) | Static scale (12-24 points) |
| **International Universities** | Direct entry | Via A-Levels | Via A-Levels usually |

### 6.2 Recognition Equivalency
```
IGCSE A* ≈ KCSE A ≈ CBC Level 7
IGCSE A  ≈ KCSE B ≈ CBC Level 6-7
IGCSE B  ≈ KCSE C ≈ CBC Level 5-6
IGCSE C  ≈ KCSE D ≈ CBC Level 4-5
IGCSE E  ≈ KCSE E ≈ CBC Level 3-4
```
